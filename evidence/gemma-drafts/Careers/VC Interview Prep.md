# VC Interview Prep

> Why VC, pitching an industry and a company, mock calls, and quick math.
> Topic: [[index#Careers|Careers]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
VC interview preparation covers several key areas, including conducting mock calls, pitching an industry and a company, and performing quick financial calculations. This preparation is designed to test your intuition and ability to evaluate opportunities from an investor's perspective.

## Key points
*   A partner may ask for a mock call during an interview to assess your ability and intuition when speaking with companies [^1].
*   In a mock call, the VC plays the role of the investor asking questions, and the entrepreneur's answers should gauge your critical thinking and investor lens [^1].
*   When discussing a company, you should be prepared to discuss the company within the context of the industry, including market size, recent funding, firms with the most investment, and positive trends or tailwinds [^2].
*   The pitch should be broken down into three parts: an introduction, tying in material events and hires to make careful assessments, and overall suggestions or questions for management [^3].
*   VCs often ask basic questions to ensure you understand the industry before moving to a company pitch [^4].
*   When probing into a company, you should continually question the business until you have a clear understanding of their product offering, sometimes using a hypothetical use case [^5].
*   When discussing numbers, you can challenge the entrepreneur's understanding of the numbers they are putting forward, especially regarding target numbers [^5].
*   Exit opportunities should be discussed by considering if a big strategic company like IBM, Johnson & Johnson, or UnitedHealth might purchase the company, or if an IPO could be possible in the next few years [^6].
