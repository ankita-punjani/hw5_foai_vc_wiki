# Venture Debt

> Loans to venture-backed companies, and when they make sense.
> Topic: [[index#Financing Types|Financing Types]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
Venture debt involves providing short-term loans to venture-backed companies as an alternative to equity financing. This allows a company to secure necessary cash flow to meet sales demands without diluting ownership equity, which can be crucial for saving equity for larger expansion initiatives.

## Key points
*   Venture debt is a method for injecting capital into an early-stage business through short-term loans to avoid further equity dilution [^1].
*   A company might request venture debt to meet sales demand so that equity can be reserved for more substantial initiatives like national expansion [^1].
*   Warrants are a clause in venture debt that gives investors the right to convert the debt into equity within a specified timeframe [^1].
*   The Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) is the return that equity owners and lenders expect from a company and is used as a discount rate in the discounted cash flow valuation method [^1].
*   Debt financing can be cheaper than equity financing because the cost of obtaining cash through debt is relatively less expensive [^2].
*   Contracts for debt financing may contain debt covenants, which are requirements a company must follow every quarter or risk defaulting on the loan [^3].
*   Firms typically only provide venture debt for companies that have balance sheet debt under 20 percent of operating expenses [^3].
