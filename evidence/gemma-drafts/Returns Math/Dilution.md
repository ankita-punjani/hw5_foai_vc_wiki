# Dilution

> How later rounds shrink ownership, and what pro-rata does about it.
> Topic: [[index#Returns Math|Returns Math]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]], [[Harlem Capital Return the Fund Post]]

## Summary
Dilution refers to the shrinking of an investor's ownership stake in a company as new funding rounds occur. Pro-rata rights are mechanisms investors use to try and maintain their original ownership percentage despite these subsequent financings.

## Key points
*   The dilution assumption is a significant guess in the return model, with examples showing total dilution by exit [^1].
*   Investors may use pro-rata rights in future rounds to prevent dilution, especially in early-stage investments [^1].
*   Assuming a Seed round is 25% and subsequent rounds step down 5% each round (20% for Series A, 15% for Series B, 10% for Series C, and 5% as a floor for Series D+), this shows the potential dilution structure [^1].
*   Investors reserve 30–40% of their fund for follow-ons to use their pro-rata rights in Series A to prevent dilution [^1].
*   Dilution can cause valuation divergence, where the actual return on investment is lower than anticipated, sometimes by 3–5x [^2].
*   Venture capitalists invest in early- to mid-stage private companies to earn large returns, with early-stage investors targeting a 3x–5x ROI [^3].
*   Pro-rata involves participation in future fundraising rounds to maintain the same stake in ownership [^2].

## Worked example
The dilution comparison shows that cutting the Series B and Series C round’s dilution by 50.0% leads to 12.1% dilution and 6.6% ownership at exit [^1]. This reduction in dilution resulted in an investor only needing a $605mm exit instead of a $700mm exit to return the fund, which equates to $95mm [^1].
