# IRR and Return Multiples

> Cash-on-cash multiples, IRR, and return expectations by stage.
> Topic: [[index#Returns Math|Returns Math]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]], [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]]

## Summary
IRR is a metric used to measure profitability over time by accounting for all cash inflows and outflows, unlike simply compounding annual growth rates. It is a key performance metric used by investors to evaluate potential returns, often over a 5 to 10-year horizon.

## Key points
*   Investors typically anticipate a 20-30 percent internal rate of return (IRR) over a 5-year investment time horizon [^1].
*   IRR is an annualized growth rate that accounts for all cash inflows and outflows within a period [^1][^2].
*   For a 5-year time horizon, a 1.5x return is associated with about a 10 percent IRR, 2x with 15 percent IRR, 3x with 25 percent IRR, 4x with about 30 percent IRR, 5x with 40 percent IRR, and 6x with 45 percent IRR [^1].
*   The Venture Capital Method uses a high discount rate to reflect investor return expectations, which drives the amount of ownership required [^3].
*   The expected time to liquidity varies by stage, for example, Seed stage expects 5 to 8 years for liquidity [^4].
*   An acceptable annual customer churn rate is anything below 7 percent, which corresponds to about a 0.58 percent monthly churn [^5].
