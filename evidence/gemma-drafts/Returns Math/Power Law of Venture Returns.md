# Power Law of Venture Returns

> Why one outlier has to pay for the whole portfolio.
> Topic: [[index#Returns Math|Returns Math]] · From: [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]], [[Harlem Capital Return the Fund Post]]

## Summary
The Power Law of Venture Returns describes the phenomenon where a small number of successful investments must generate the majority of the fund's returns, meaning a few outliers must cover the losses incurred by the rest of the portfolio. This structure is inherent in venture investing, where the success of the entire fund often hinges on the performance of a select few companies.

## Key points
*   A goal for an early-stage fund is often set at achieving a 3x gross return [^1][^2].
*   When 10 companies are in a portfolio, 5 companies going bankrupt results in a cost of 0.4x [^1][^2].
*   If 1 company provides all the returns, the required multiple is 34x [^1][^2].
*   The same scenario (10 companies, 5 bankrupt) results in a cost of 0.2x [^2].
*   The multiple required for the return is 21x in one scenario [^1], and 34x in another [^2].
*   Equal weighting suggests using half the initial investment and a 200% reserve for non-losers [^1].
*   A fund may need billions in value creation to meet 3x-5x return goals, even if unicorns are not strictly necessary [^3].
