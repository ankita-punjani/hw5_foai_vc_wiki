# Venture Fund Structure

> GPs, LPs, fund life, raising the next fund, and angels vs VCs.
> Topic: [[index#Fund Structure|Fund Structure]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
Venture fund structure involves various parties such as General Partners (GPs) and Limited Partners (LPs), the lifecycle of a fund, and the distinction between venture capital and angel investing. This structure dictates how capital is raised, how investments are managed, and how returns are distributed.

## Key points
*   Limited Partners include endowments, foundations, public pensions, and high net worth individuals who fund venture capital firms [^1].
*   Venture capital is often allocated as a portion of funds within a category of alternative investments, such as private equity and venture capital [^2].
*   Venture capital firms may spend 3-5 years investing and 5 more years (8 to 10 years in total) to recoup money and generate a return [^3].
*   Venture capitalists primarily invest other people's money, unlike angel investors who invest their own money [^4].
*   The average fund size for venture capital stages is about $400M, and the average venture capital deal size is $18M [^3].
*   The larger the firm’s stake in a company, the larger the returns will be upon an exit through an IPO or M&A activity [^3].
*   Venture capitalists charge a management fee of about 2 percent to the limited partners to cover salaries and support staff [^3].

## Worked example
The average fund size for venture capital stages is about $400M [^3].

This indicates that a typical investment might involve a deal size of $18M [^3].
