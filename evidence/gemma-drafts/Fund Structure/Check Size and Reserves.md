# Check Size and Reserves

> Typical check sizes and holding money back for follow-on rounds.
> Topic: [[index#Fund Structure|Fund Structure]] · From: [[Harlem Capital Return the Fund Post]]

## Summary
This topic covers the typical size of checks used in venture capital funds and the practice of holding back capital for follow-on investments. Fund check sizes depend on the fund's size and strategy, while reserves are crucial for maintaining ownership stakes in portfolio companies during subsequent funding rounds.

## Key points
*   A typical check size range is determined by the fund's size and strategy, such as diversified versus concentrated investing [^1].
*   Seed valuations are determined by ownership targets rather than revenue multiples [^1].
*   Larger funds ($100mm+) generally target 10% to 20% ownership, while microfunds ($25mm – $100mm) target 5% to 10% ownership [^1].
*   Investors typically reserve 30% to 40% of their fund for follow-on investments to secure pro-rata rights in winners [^2].
*   Assuming a Seed round is 25% dilution, investors usually assume 25% to 30% dilution by exit, with the Series A pro-rata check [^2].
*   A fund may not need unicorns to return the fund, but it may need billions in value creation to meet 3x-5x return goals [^3].
*   A fund is generally not expected to invest less than 1% of the fund in any company [^3].
