# Pre-Money and Post-Money Valuation

> Pre-money + money = post-money, and how ownership follows.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
Pre-money and post-money valuations define the value of a company at different points in a financing round. The pre-money valuation is the company's value before the investment, while the post-money valuation is the value after the investment has been made. These valuations are crucial for determining the ownership stake investors receive.

## Key points
*   The pre-money valuation is the value of a company before the latest round of venture capital has been invested [^1].
*   The post-money valuation is the value of a company immediately after the latest amount of venture capital is invested [^1].
*   The key difference between pre- and post-money valuation lies in the timing of the valuation and the resulting ownership stake [^1].
*   If the pre- and post-money valuations are the same and the amount invested is the same, the entrepreneur has relatively less equity and the venture capitalist has relatively more equity in the post-money valuation [^2].
*   The amount invested in a pre-money example can represent 1/npre of the total, where 1/npre equals the proportion of the company the investor owns after adding the investment [^3].
*   If the pre-money investment represents 20 percent (1/5th), the post-money investment should represent 25 percent (1/4th) of the total [^3].
*   If a company has a $500k post-money valuation and an investment of $250k, the investor owns 50 percent of the company [^4].
*   If a company has a $500k pre-money valuation and an investment of $250k, the investor owns approximately 33.3 percent of the company [^4].

## Worked example
If an investment of $250k is made at a pre-money valuation of $1M, the total capital becomes $1.25M, resulting in 20 percent ownership for investors [^3].
Alternatively, if an investment of $500k is made at a pre-money valuation of $1M, the total capital is $1.5M, and investors own about 33.3 percent of the company [^3].
