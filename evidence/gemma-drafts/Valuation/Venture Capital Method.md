# Venture Capital Method

> Discount a hoped-for exit by the required return to price a round today.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]], [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]]

## Summary
The Venture Capital (VC) method values a company by discounting a hoped-for exit value by the required return to determine a fair price for the investment today. This method relies heavily on investor return expectations to set the valuation.

## Key points
*   The VC method is rooted by experienced investors who have been investing in early-stage companies for over 20 years [^1].
*   Precedent transactions analysis involves creating a universe of completed deals within the past five years to find an industry average multiple [^2].
*   The VC method can be used to back out a post-money valuation based on the anticipated enterprise value and ROI in the exit year [^2].
*   Anticipated seed stage ROI is typically in the ballpark of 30x, compared to maybe 5-7x at the growth stage [^3].
*   The VC method includes growth, current and future cash needs, and the fundraising environment in its consideration [^4].
*   A key activity in the VC method is to figure out how much money is needed today and estimate the final enterprise value if things go well [^5].
*   The VC method requires using a high discount rate for the investor’s return expectation [^5].

## Worked example
The VC method can be used to calculate a valuation based on an anticipated exit.
If a company has an enterprise value at an exit of $84M and a target ROI at an exit of 30x, this is used in the formula [^3].
If a healthcare startup is asking for an investment of $500k, this gives them a pre-money valuation of $2.2M [^3].
