# Projection Reflection

> Price today by discounting the likely next-round valuation.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]], [[Harlem Capital Return the Fund Post]]

## Summary
Projection Reflection is a method used in venture capital to estimate a company's valuation by discounting the likely valuation of a future funding round. This technique helps in understanding the potential impact of various assumptions on the company's worth. It is used to create a language for partnership discussions by setting shared expectations between investors and management.

## Key points
*   Optimal financings often occur following major step functions [^1].
*   Valuation changes are described as occurring in "step-functions" rather than linearly [^1].
*   When operating without shared understanding, the goal is to operate the business to maximize the probability and value of raising money [^1].
*   Scenario analysis involves estimating various variables such as premoney and money, exit scenarios, potential outcomes and growth, probabilities of those outcomes, and dilution from future financings [^2].
*   Scenario analysis steps include picking end state scenarios, considering the dilution of later rounds, determining probabilities and time, and analyzing the resulting valuation and risk profile [^2].
*   Investors may require a certain ownership percentage, such as 15% ownership based on similar VC methods [^3].
*   Founders should run calculations to understand what investors are assuming for an exit, especially when considering dilution, as investors take a piece of the company [^4].

## Worked example
If the next round is likely to be $30m pre-money, the calculation for the post-money valuation is as follows:
$30m / 2.5 = $12m post-money valuation [^1].
Then, $12m post - $5m = $7m pre-money valuation [^1].
