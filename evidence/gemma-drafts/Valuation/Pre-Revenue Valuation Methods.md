# Pre-Revenue Valuation Methods

> Berkus, Chicago, risk-factor summation, and other rules of thumb.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
Pre-revenue valuation methods provide different frameworks for assessing the worth of early-stage startups, often relying on investor expertise and risk factors. These methods include the Berkus Method, the Risk Factor Summation Method, and rules of thumb that help investors determine a fair pre-money valuation.

## Key points
*   The Berkus Method can yield a maximum pre-money valuation of $2.5M, but only $2M is considered for a pre-revenue business due to the risk of not adding value in category 5 [^1].
*   The Risk Factor Summation Method focuses on the overall risk profile using a broad set of factors and a median pre-revenue valuation that is region-specific [^2].
*   The Risk Factor Summation Method maps out 12 risks, which are assessed with values ranging from -2 (Very negative) to +2 (Very positive) for growing the company [^2].
*   Using Jeffrey Sohl’s 2015 data, an average pre-money valuation for pre-revenue deals can be $2M [^2].
*   The Risk Factor Summation Method calculates a net increment by adding $250k for every +1 risk factor and decrementing $250k for every -1 risk factor [^2].
*   The Rule of Thirds is a screening tactic that allocates one-third of the company to pre-revenue investors, another third for founders, and the remaining third for future C-level executives and management [^3].
*   Pre-money valuations for pre-revenue businesses are typically in the ballpark of $2M to $3M, based on the expertise of seasoned investors [^4].

## Worked example
Using the Risk Factor Summation Method, an average pre-money valuation of $2M can be established [^2]. If this average is used, adding $500k to it results in a $2.5M pre-money valuation for a pre-revenue company [^2].
