# Comparables Valuation

> Pricing a company from multiples of similar public firms or deals.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]], [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]]

## Summary
Comparables valuation involves pricing a company by looking at multiples derived from similar public firms or past transactions. This technique helps determine a company's value by applying established ratios to comparable companies to estimate its worth.

## Key points
* Precedent transactions analysis (PTA) uses completed deals within the past five years to find an industry average multiple [^1].
* When using precedent transactions analysis, the product of the multiple and the target company’s EBITDA or sales will give the enterprise value [^1].
* Precedent transactions analysis tends to result in a larger valuation because firms are usually sold at a premium, known as a control premium [^1].
* Valuation methods must incorporate at least SIX concepts [^2].
* Analysts use EBITDA instead of net income because it allows for a comparison of "apples to apples" across different companies [^3].
* The multiple (EV/EBITDA) shows how many times the market values the company's earnings, for example, 5x means the market values every $1 this company makes as if it were $5 [^3].
* To estimate an enterprise value using revenue, you can determine the median revenue in the exit year from similar companies and attribute it to the target company [^4].

## Worked example
If a sample company has an enterprise value of $100 and an EBITDA of $20, the company’s multiple (EV/EBITDA) is 5x [^3].
