# Valuation Fundamentals

> Value vs price vs valuation, and why VC valuation is an art.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]]

## Summary
Valuation involves determining the worth of a company, which is described as both an art and a science. Venture capital valuation is particularly challenging because methods are precise but not entirely accurate, and the assumptions used can significantly impact the final result.

## Key points
*   VC valuation is considered an art, not a science, meaning there are no definitive right answers [^1].
*   Methods used in VC valuation are described as "precise" but not "accurate," and assumptions are a key factor that can overwhelm the mathematical calculations [^1][^2].
*   Traditional valuation methods include Comparables, Net Present Value (NPV), and options analysis [^2].
*   VC valuation methods must incorporate at least six concepts [^3].
*   Comparables involve looking at similar private deals or ratios of similar public firms, such as market value/sales or market value/profits (P/E) [^4].
*   The VC Method involves four essential activities: figuring out how much money is needed today, estimating the final "enterprise value" if things go well, using a high discount rate based on investor return expectations, and adjusting for later financing rounds [^5].
