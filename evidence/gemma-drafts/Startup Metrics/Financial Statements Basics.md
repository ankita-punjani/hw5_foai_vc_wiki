# Financial Statements Basics

> Income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement at interview depth.
> Topic: [[index#Startup Metrics|Startup Metrics]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
Financial statements provide a snapshot of a company's financial health through three main statements: the income statement, the cash flow statement, and the balance sheet. Understanding how these statements connect is crucial for analyzing a company's performance.

## Key points
*   The acronym ICB stands for Income Statement $\rightarrow$ Cash Flow Statement $\rightarrow$ Balance Sheet [^1].
*   The income statement starts with revenue and subtracts expenses like Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), Selling, General, and Administrative (SG&A), and Research and Development (R&D) to reach EBITDA [^2].
*   EBITDA is calculated after subtracting depreciation and amortization from the result of subtracting COGS and OPEX [^3].
*   Cash flow from operations (CFO) includes Net Income, changes in working capital, and adjustments for non-cash items like depreciation [^4].
*   Depreciation is a theoretical measure of asset decay, and it is added back into CFO because it does not involve real cash movement [^4].
*   The balance sheet shows what a company owns (Assets) and what it owes (Liabilities), with Assets equaling Liabilities plus Shareholders’ Equity [^5].
*   Retained earnings are calculated by taking original retained earnings, adding net income, and subtracting dividends [^6].

## Worked example
If a company bought something for $1000 and decided to sell it for $500, the depreciation calculation would be $100 dollars, assuming a five-year useful life [^3].
