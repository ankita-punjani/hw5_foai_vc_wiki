# Growth Equity

> Later-round equity for companies heading to IPO or acquisition.
> Topic: [[index#Financing Types|Financing Types]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
Growth equity involves investing in later-stage companies, such as Series C, D, or E rounds, with the goal of helping them achieve an Initial Public Offering (IPO) or secure an acquisition. These investments focus on companies that are leaders or emerging leaders in their industry and are on the path to becoming profitable.

## Key points
* Growth equity investments can range from $10M to $500M or higher, depending on whether growth equity firms experiment with majority buyouts [^1].
* Target companies for growth equity should have at least $5M in revenue, 20 percent revenue growth in the last few years or higher, a proven business model, and a clear pathway to becoming profitable [^1].
* Growth equity investments are typically minority investments, meaning the venture firm takes less than 50 percent in the company, to leave equity for future financing rounds [^2].
* Growth equity investments are usually directed toward businesses that are leaders or emerging leaders in their industry, have a few million in revenue, high gross margins, and are on their way to becoming profitable [^3].
* Private equity firms often take a 100 percent stake in an operational company and scale it through add-on acquisitions and organic growth over 4 to 7 years before selling [^2].
* Growth equity firms like Stripes Group, General Atlantic, and Insight Venture Partners typically enter at the Series C or D stage when a business is the number one or two player in the industry and is ready for an IPO or strategic acquisition [^1].
* Private equity firms rely on a leveraged buyout (LBO) model where they use a large loan to purchase a company, with cash making up 30 to 40 percent of the investment [^4].
