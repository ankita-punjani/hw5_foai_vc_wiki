# Management Fees and Carried Interest

> The ~2% management fee and 20% carried interest.
> Topic: [[index#Fund Structure|Fund Structure]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]], [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]]

## Summary
Partners who run a venture fund (GPs) earn money two ways: an annual management fee paid by the limited partners, and carried interest, a share of the profits after the LPs have their money back.

## Key points
* Partners charge a management fee of about 2% to the limited partners, which pays their own salaries and the salaries of associates and support staff [^1].
* Once the limited partners receive 100% of their money back, the partners receive 20% of any additional profits and the LPs receive 80% [^1].
* That 20% share is called carried interest; the glossary defines it as the profits partners receive after the amount invested has been returned, commonly 20% of future profits in VC [^1][^2].
* Fees shrink what gets invested: in the Haas example a fund of 1x pays 0.2x in fees, leaving 0.8x to invest [^3].

## Worked example
1. Haas slide: total fund 1x → fees 0.2x → 0.8x actually invested [^3].
1. To hit a 3x gross return on the whole fund, the 0.8x invested must grow to 3x, which is why the winners have to be so large (see [[Power Law of Venture Returns]]) [^3].
