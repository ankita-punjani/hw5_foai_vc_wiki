# Check Size and Reserves

> Typical check sizes and holding money back for follow-on rounds.
> Topic: [[index#Fund Structure|Fund Structure]] · From: [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]], [[Harlem Capital Return the Fund Post]]

## Summary
A fund's typical check size follows from its size and strategy (diversified vs concentrated). Funds also hold back reserves: money kept for follow-on checks in later rounds, so they can defend their ownership in the companies that are working.

## Key points
* Every fund has a typical check size range based on fund size and strategy (diversified vs concentrated); founders should ask early [^1].
* Example (one seed microfund, Harlem Capital): a $40mm fund investing in ~30 companies writes $500k to $1mm checks, with an average check of $750k [^1].
* A fund generally won't invest less than 1% of the fund in any company, which is why a $250mm seed fund is likely to write checks of $2mm+ [^2].
* Funds hold back **reserves** for follow-on checks. The example fund keeps 30–40% of the fund to take its pro-rata in winners at the Series A and prevent dilution; every fund's reserves differ, so founders should ask upfront [^3].
* The Haas slides make the same point: reserve dollars matter, and at early stage reserves can exceed 100% of the initial investment [^4].
* In the slides' portfolio example, putting in half the initial check and a 200% reserve for non-losers cuts the multiple the winner must return from 34x to 21x [^5].

## Worked example
1. Example fund: $40mm spread over ~30 companies, average first check $750k [^1].
1. Rule of thumb: minimum check ≈ 1% of the fund, so $40mm → $400k and $250mm → $2.5mm (my arithmetic from the 1% rule [^2]).
1. Reserves: 30–40% of $40mm = $12mm–$16mm held for follow-on (pro-rata) checks (my arithmetic from [^3]).
