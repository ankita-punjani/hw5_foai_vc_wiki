# Venture Fund Structure

> GPs, LPs, fund life, raising the next fund, and angels vs VCs.
> Topic: [[index#Fund Structure|Fund Structure]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
A venture fund raises money from limited partners (LPs) such as endowments and pension funds. The partners who run it (GPs) invest that money in startups over a multi-year fund life, and aim to return it with a profit. Angels are different: they invest their own money.

## Key points
* Limited partners are the endowments, foundations, public pensions and high-net-worth individuals that fund venture capital firms [^1].
* LPs invest in VC to diversify: portfolio managers put a slice of the portfolio, usually under 15%, into alternative investments (private equity and VC) because VC is so risky [^2].
* A fund spends 3–5 years investing and about 5 more years (8 to 10 years in total) getting the LPs' money back plus a return [^3].
* Firms raise the next fund when about 75% of the current fund is committed, usually 5–7 years into its life, so there is always capital for new and follow-on deals [^3].
* Per the book, the average fund size across VC stages is about $400M and the average VC deal size is $18M [^3].
* Angel investors invest their own money; venture capitalists primarily invest other people's money [^4].
* Partners are paid through a ~2% management fee and 20% carried interest (see [[Management Fees and Carried Interest]]) [^3].

## Worked example
1. The book's example: Cynthia and Mercedes pitch a college endowment and a public pension fund, which agree to invest, and form CynMerc Ventures [^3].
1. Fund I invests for 3–5 years, then spends ~5 more years returning the LPs' money plus a return (8–10 years total) [^3].
1. When ~75% of Fund I is committed (5–7 years in), they raise CynMerc II so they never run out of committed capital [^3].
