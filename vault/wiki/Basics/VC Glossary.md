# VC Glossary

> Plain definitions of the terms every other note uses.
> Topic: [[index#Basics|Basics]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]], [[Harlem Capital Return the Fund Post]]

## Summary
Plain-English definitions of the terms used across this wiki, quoted or closely paraphrased from the book's glossary and chapters. Where a definition is one author's convention rather than a universal rule, that is flagged.

## Key points
* **Accelerator:** works with a company at the earliest stage, usually for a fixed term, with mentorship and education, ending in a pitch to investors [^1].
* **Alternative investments:** assets other than traditional stocks or bonds (e.g. private equity, venture capital, real estate), usually held by large organizations like pension funds [^1].
* **Angel investor:** an individual investing their own money, often one of the earliest investors, before a sellable product exists [^2].
* **Burn rate:** the amount of venture capital cash a startup uses ("burns") in a period, usually per month [^1].
* **Capitalization (cap) table:** the list of all shareholders in a private company, with their percentage ownership and equity value in each round [^1].
* **Carried interest (carry):** the share of profits the partners receive after the amount invested has been returned; commonly 20% of future profits [^1].
* **Committed capital:** money a limited partner has promised to a venture firm; the general partner requests it when the firm decides to invest [^1].
* **Dilution:** a reduction in ownership (or value) between financing rounds [^1]. See [[Dilution]].
* **Enterprise value:** the value of the entire firm; what valuation techniques try to estimate [^1].
* **Fund returner:** one investment that pays back 100% of the whole fund by itself [^3]. See [[Return the Fund]].
* **Growth equity:** minority investments in late-stage companies (Series C, D, E…) aiming for an IPO or acquisition [^1]. See [[Growth Equity]].
* **IPO (initial public offering):** the first time a private company issues stock to the public on an exchange [^1].
* **IRR (internal rate of return):** an annualized growth rate that accounts for cash in and out over the period; used to evaluate portfolios over 5–10 years [^1]. See [[IRR and Return Multiples]].
* **Limited partners (LPs):** the endowments, foundations, public pensions and high-net-worth individuals that fund venture capital firms [^1].
* **Management fee:** the fee (about 2%) partners charge limited partners to pay the firm's salaries [^4]. See [[Management Fees and Carried Interest]].
* **M&A (mergers & acquisitions):** one business buying or merging with another using debt or equity [^1].
* **Pre-money / post-money valuation:** the company's value just before / just after the new round's money comes in [^1]. See [[Pre-Money and Post-Money Valuation]].
* **Pro-rata:** follow-on investment in later rounds to keep the same ownership stake [^1].
* **ROI (return on investment):** a target multiple of cash invested; the glossary gives 8–10x for early-stage and 3–5x for late-stage investors [^1]. (The book's VC-method chapter uses ~30x as a typical seed target [^6]; see [[Venture Capital Method]].)
* **Runway:** the venture capital still left in a company's bank account [^1].
* **Seed round:** the first round of funding, often friends, family, angels and other early-stage investors [^1].
* **TAM (total addressable market):** the size of the market the company is entering or already in [^1].
* **Unicorn:** a technology company valued at over $1B [^1].
* **Valuation divergence:** as a startup's value rises, early private shares rise much more slowly, cutting early investors' cash-on-cash ROI by as much as 3–5x [^1].
* **Venture capital:** money an early-stage company receives to grow, from investors who pool money from limited partners [^5]. See [[How Venture Capital Works]].
* **Venture Capital Method:** estimate the company's value at exit, divide by the target ROI to get today's post-money valuation, then subtract the investment to get pre-money [^1]. See [[Venture Capital Method]].
* **Venture debt:** short-term loans to an early-stage business, so it can raise cash without further diluting equity [^1]. See [[Venture Debt]].
* **Warrants:** a clause giving investors the right to convert debt into equity within a set time frame [^1].
