# Ownership Targets

> Seed valuations are set by the ownership a fund needs, not revenue.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]], [[Harlem Capital Return the Fund Post]]

## Summary
At seed, a round's price is set by how much of the company the fund needs to own, not by revenue multiples. Check size ÷ ownership target gives the post-money valuation a fund can accept.

## Key points
* Seed valuations are NOT determined by revenue multiples but by ownership targets [^1].
* Founders should expect to give up 20%–30% ownership in the seed round [^1].
* Larger funds ($100mm+) generally target 10%–20% ownership; microfunds ($25mm–$100mm) target 5%–10% [^1].
* Leading a round usually means a bigger stake: one microfund (Harlem Capital) targets 5%–7.5% when co-investing and 7.5%–10% when it leads [^1].
* A $750k check at those targets means only investing at a post-money valuation of $7.5mm to $15mm [^1].
* The Haas slides show the same idea as a negotiation point: "We typically require 20% of the company…", asking whether that is a requirement or a negotiation [^2].

## Worked example
1. $750k ÷ 10% = $7.5mm post-money; $750k ÷ 5% = $15mm post-money [^1].
1. At a $10mm valuation, a founder giving up 20%–30% implies a $2mm–$3mm seed round [^1].
