# Valuation Fundamentals

> Value vs price vs valuation, and why VC valuation is an art.
> Topic: [[index#Valuation|Valuation]] · From: [[Haas Venture Valuation Slides]]

## Summary
Value, price, and valuation are different things: value depends on who is looking, price is what two parties agree on, and valuation is the analysis that informs the negotiation. Startup valuation is an art, not a science, because VC-backed companies lack the data traditional methods need.

## Key points
* Value is in the eye of the beholder (a utility function); price is a negotiated value between two parties; valuation (the activity) is an estimate that informs the negotiation of price. In VC, "valuation" (the noun) usually means price paid [^1].
* A deal happens because buyers think value is higher than sellers do; value depends on the economic actor [^2].
* Traditional methods need markets, ongoing operations, cash flows and a debt/equity capital structure. VC-funded companies have emerging markets, changing business models, negative cash flows, no borrowing capacity, preferred stock with liquidation preferences, and future rounds [^3].
* So VC valuation is "art, not science": there are no right answers, methods are "precise" but not "accurate", and assumptions can overwhelm the math [^4].
* Valuation methods must incorporate at least six concepts, including cash efficiency, risk of failure, stage of growth (value moves in step functions), competitive context, and the type of investor [^5].
* Value depends heavily on deal specifics, industry, size of the raise, company quality, supply and demand, public-market expectations, and fit for the firm, portfolio and dealmaker [^6].

## Worked example
1. Comparing the VC valuations of one company (Mekasutra Games) from three methods shows the "precise but not accurate" point: VC method $9.6m, Projection Reflection $7m, Scenario Analysis $4.5m+ pre-money [^7].
