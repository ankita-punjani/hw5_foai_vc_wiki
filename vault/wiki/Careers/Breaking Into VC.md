# Breaking Into VC

> Networking, standing out, clubs, and advice from analysts who broke in.
> Topic: [[index#Careers|Careers]] · From: [[Break Into VC Book]]

## Summary
Breaking into venture capital requires proactive effort in networking, demonstrating unique value beyond a standard resume, and showing genuine passion for the industry. Success often depends on building connections with various industry players and proving your ability to engage with entrepreneurs and portfolio companies.

## Key points
*   Focusing too much on being formal can be a mistake when recruiting for venture capital jobs [^1].
*   To differentiate yourself, show a project or pitch deck rather than relying solely on the resume [^1].
*   Emphasize a vertical of interest on your resume, noting what you learned from a single project to show you can break down companies better than others [^1].
*   Highlighting experience with a company, club, or startup should be emphasized at the top of your materials [^2].
*   Proving you can do the work of a VC involves offering suggestions for portfolio companies you believe a VC should look at [^2].
*   Networking is crucial, as it provides opportunities to learn about different industries and deal stages by talking to people in the VC landscape [^3].
*   Reaching out directly to people, rather than relying on job postings, is more effective for securing opportunities [^4].
*   It is important to show you have been a leader in some capacity, such as through a club or off-campus activity [^5].
*   Starting a student club, such as a venture group, can help separate yourself from others [^6].
